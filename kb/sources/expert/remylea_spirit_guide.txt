# Definitions
I should also go over some terminology that I will be using frequently throughout the Spirit Guide. Below are explanations for each of those terms:
Pallet control: When you have “pallet control”, this means that you are standing in an undropped pallet with the survivor on some other part/side of the tile. If you have “pallet control” this means you have the ability to block the pallet with your husk, preventing the survivor from dropping it.
Husk Placement: “Husk placement” is the proper place to set up your husk before you begin to phase (i.e., to block a pallet or window, to allow yourself to use the least amount of your phase possible so your power recharges faster, or to allow yourself to use the full duration of your phase to its maximum potential). 
Pause point: A “pause point” is the place where you should briefly pause while phasing to listen and identify where the survivor is and what they’re doing. Once you’ve done this, your placement in this pause point will not only zone them away from resources but put them in a position so it doesn’t matter what they choose to do, you’ll be able to effectively react and get the hit almost every time. The “pause point” is also often in a good place to mind game the survivor, potentially tricking them into vaulting a pallet and getting stuck between your husk and the pallet.
- Remember: It’s important to be patient and listen to where the survivor is and what they’re doing in a proper pause point before coming out of phase. If you’re husk is properly blocking a pallet, for example, and you choose to short phase to where you think the survivor is, rather than be patient and listen in a proper pause point, you run the risk of them making it back to the pallet or being able to play said pallet without getting the hit.
Spirit God Pallet: Spirit can play around a lot of pallets that would be classified as ‘God pallets’ to other killers (i.e., God Rocks on Macmillan), but there are some that she cannot. A God pallet for spirit classifies as a pallet that Spirit is not able to phase to the other side of and back to where she left her husk. This is because, even if she sets up her husk to block the pallet from one side and is able to phase to the other side of the pallet (forcing the survivor to vault it and become stuck between her husk and the pallet), the survivor will be able to become unstuck before she is able to get back to them, allowing the survivor to freely vault the pallet again or potentially move to another resource. If a survivor throws a Spirit God pallet, you must break it. Don’t waste time (or your power) trying to play around it.
3 or 4 Gen: A “3 Gen” describes 3 uncompleted generators that are relatively close to one another. Similarly, a “4 Gen” describes 4 uncompleted generators that are relatively close to one another. I’ll talk more about this in Spirit Tips and Tricks for Playstyle but you will often want to keep in mind the generators you have that are close together, i.e., your 3 or 4 Gens, as Spirit thrives in defending a specific area of a given map.
Snowballing/Snowball Potential: Snowballing describes creating enough pressure in the match to turn the game heavily in your favour (and potentially end it quickly). For instance, if you have a survivor on hook in the corner of the map and then you’re able to get a couple hits on two other survivors as they try to come in for the unhook, they realize they won’t be able to make it, so they run away and the survivor on hook goes to second stage. You then leave the area to pursue the survivors you just injured and get a down. In this scenario, you have A LOT of pressure and potential to end the game. Say then a generator gets completed and you know it had to be the last remaining healthy survivor that finished it, you go over there and find the healthy survivor healing the other injured survivor. You can now down the injured survivor and pursue the healthy survivor to down them before they can make it to the unhook or pick up one of the downed survivors. Spirit has amazing snow ball potential since she can punish survivors very easily for being caught out of position (not immediately beside a resource) and giving unnecessary hits and downs, especially if they’re in bad areas (i.e., taking a hit in a spot where she knows she’ll be able to follow up to get a quick down, or going down in an area of generators that she wants to defend). There are perks that help increase your snowball potential, such as anti-healing perks like Sloppy Butcher, perks that will guarantee you to have a hook in a favourable spot like Agitation, or perks that could incentivize survivors to work gens injured instead of healing like Hex: Ruin.
# Spirit Tips and Tricks
## FOV
- Behaviour introduced an FOV slider into the game that you can find in settings under ‘Beta’ and it’s important to be aware that your FOV does affect the audio of your game, which of course, will affect your Spirit gameplay. Why does FOV affect your audio you might ask? Essentially, imagine that your FOV is a bubble around your killer’s head. When your FOV is increased, that ‘bubble’ is just getting pushed further and further behind your character to increase what they’re able to see, and your audio unfortunately is also tied to it. That being said, a higher FOV makes Spirit’s audio a bit more ‘distorted’ because the survivors and every other sound in the game will sound closer or more behind you than it actually is. This will begin to apply for any FOV above 97. 
- You can still use a high FOV. Myself and many other Spirit players have or do play on the maximum amount of FOV available (103) and in my personal opinion, it can feel worth playing on a higher FOV so that you have the biggest advantage in what you can see plus you have the placebo affect of feeling faster. Because of the difference in audio though, I would say that an FOV of 96 or 97 is the best range to use for Spirit – this way you still have a relatively high FOV but you’re not dealing with as much distortion as I mentioned. Bottom line though, if you want to switch your FOV to something different from what you learned Spirit on, you will need to be prepared to relearn your muscle memory for audio whenever you decide to change it.
## Pallets
- One of the biggest tips I can provide to newer Spirit players is try to limit how much you’re breaking pallets. Spirit doesn’t need to break about 90% of pallets when she’s chasing as in most cases, dropped pallets, unless they’re a god pallet meaning you physically can’t get around it, can mean a guaranteed hit on the survivor if you play it correctly. Aside from that, Spirit is extremely slow and only has so many seconds in phase to work with. So, a lot of the time, all breaking a pallet does is allow the survivor to make distance to another resource since you more than likely won’t be able to catch them before hand or you’ll use your entire phase just to catch up. See How to Block Pallets and Windows. The ONLY time you should be breaking a pallet is if:
- You’re waiting for power cooldown. 
- Even here though, there are a lot of cases where it’s better to just keep the survivor zoned at the dropped pallet and just wait for your power to come back. If there are other strong resources nearby, just wait for your power and keep the survivor at the already dropped pallet.
- It’s a Spirit God pallet.
- You’re trying to get rid of stronger resources in a 3 or 4 Gen that you’re defending, but again, you typically don’t want to be doing this in chase. See Playstyle.
## Audio Settings
- Headphones in-game setting: Turn off the ‘Headphones’ setting in the Audio panel of DBD. This option tries to make your game have more ‘surround sound’ so the left and right audio distinction is less clear.
- Equalizer: Install an audio equalizer! I use the Razer Synapse equalizer but there are various great equalizers out there. Another good one is APO Peace. Here are the settings I use if you’d like to replicate them in your own equalizer settings:
The basic guidelines you want to abide by when adjusting your equalizer settings is:
- If your equalizer doesn’t already have them pre-setup, make sure you set up your equalizer to have frequencies that match the game. For instance:
Razer Synapse (Pre-Setup):
APO Peace (Manual Setup):
- You want the basses to be turned down (anything below 250 Hz). These frequencies being turned down will help reduce how overpowering basses are in game audio (dbd is a very base-heavy game), such as chase music, background audio, and explosions/noise notifications.
- 250Hz is partially responsible for controlling chase volume, however it’s also partially responsible for hearing some footstep noises, so you don’t want this to be too high or too low. Keep it around 0. 
- You want the mids to upper mids to be tuned up (anything 500Hz and above). These frequencies being turned up will help you hear higher pitched frequencies like breathing, as well as vocals and higher pitched breathy noises in grunts of pain.
- If your equalizer has a setting for it, make sure you’re on “stereo”, not “spatial audio”.
- Audio Quality Level: By default, your audio quality level in dead by daylight is set to 0. The best audio quality level for playing Spirit is 4. This is the highest AQL you can set your game to – anything above this won’t apply anything (it will act as 0) and can actually cause your game to have frame drops since you’re trying to apply audio settings that the game hasn’t configured (so do not set it above 4). To change your AQL use the following:Windows + R, Localappdata, DeadByDaylight, Saved, Config, WindowsClient, GameUserSettings, search for “Audio Quality Level:” and set the number to “4”.
- Do you need an expensive headset to play Spirit? This is a question I get a lot and the short answer is no! Having a good headset is more of a quality-of-life thing than anything else. My first 500-1k hours on Spirit I used a shitty $20 Walmart headset and I was still able to learn Spirit completely fine. Having a good headset or installing an equalizer (or other audio software) is something to consider if you plan to play Spirit religiously long-term or at a competitive level, but again, it’s more for your own quality of life than a “necessity”. 
- Please be gentle with your hearing. Your ears are a very fragile organ and can be easily damaged, so do not just increase your volume to 100% so you can hear things better. Use these tips and find a good medium that works for you, without putting your hearing at risk long term.
## Playstyle 
### Beginning of the Match
- Depending on where you spawn at the beginning of the match and what map you’re on, you can typically estimate where the survivors have spawned as well. For instance, if you spawn in a corner of a map, there’s a good chance that at least one or two survivors spawned in the opposite corner across from you. If you spawn in the center of a map, there’s a good chance that the survivors spawned apart from each other with one in each corner or two in one corner and two in another. Of course, these are only estimates, but it will typically allow you to find at least one survivor rather quickly. 
- Because of how lethal Spirit is in chase, there’s a good chance that whoever you find first, you’ll be able to down rather quickly so long as they’re not surrounded by various God pallets. Having a good start to a game (a good start being, getting your first down or multiple survivors injured quickly) is something that Spirit excels in doing consistently and is always very good for setting up the rest of the game for you. The survivors most typically won’t have a lot of progress on generators by this point and if you’re running perks like Corrupt Intervention, they definitely won’t have much progress (since they’ll have to travel across the map just to find an uncorrupted gen to start working on). 
- Once you have your first down, you can then start taking control of the game. If the down comes in super quick and in an area where you know there’s no gen progress (for example, if the survivor went down where your gens were corrupted), its typically best for you to hook them and go pressure a new survivor off one of the progressed gens across the map (especially one that connects multiple 3 of 4 gens, such as a middle gen). If the survivor goes down in or near an area where there is gen progress, try to hook them as close or as deep in this area as possible. This will allow you to pressure the surrounding area of progressed gens, as well as the unhook.
- With that being said, be mindful of where you’re deciding to take your first chase. Ideally you want to chase a survivor that is in a good area for you (i.e., not within your corrupted gens if you have Corrupt Intervention or not in an area with very little gens/a lot of strong resources) so that you can get a fast first down that you can also realistically defend/return back to quickly.
- Be mindful of any potential 3 or 4 gens on the map right from the beginning of the game (and within this, “important” gens – easily accessible gens and/or gens that connect multiple 3 or 4 gens. You will eventually want to play into these if the game doesn’t end super quickly, so you need to make sure that you’re not going to be in a situation where all of your 3 gens have been broken or that an important gen isn’t completed first without some kind of benefit to you (i.e. a tunnel out or someone put on death hook early into the game). If survivors are actively pressuring important gens right from the beginning of the match, commit chase on the survivor off the generator so that you can hopefully get a down and hook in that area or at least gain some free injuries. 
### Map Mobility
- It’s important to remember that Spirit isn't good at map mobility as she cannot use her power for chase and then immediately use it for mobility and vice versa. 
- The only time you should be using the entirety of your phase to traverse the map is if you have to get somewhere relatively far away as soon as possible or if you know you’ll still have some distance to walk and allow your power to recharge before reaching that destination. More often than not, however, it is much smarter to only use about half of your power bar for traversing and walk the rest of the distance so that once you locate a survivor you want to chase, you can instantly use your phase on them. 
- Using addons that increase Spirit’s movement speed can, however, make her better at map mobility. This is not merely because of the increase in speed, but because being faster in phase means you won’t have to consume as much of your power to cover the same amount of distance, which means your power bar will have less to recharge (and therefore, you’ll be able to phase again quicker). The addons that increase Spirit’s movement speed are Muddy Sports Cap (10% increase) and Mother Daughter Ring (25% increase). Mother Daughter Ring is the one that will really have an impact with map mobility though. 
### Area Defense and Hook Placement
- Even though Spirit isn’t great with map mobility, she does, however, excel at defending a certain area of the map. It’s important that you keep an eye on where your closest gens are when you load into the match so you can play into potential 3 or 4 Gens.
- Depending on the current situation of the match (i.e, you have a hook in a good set up of gens), a good strategy might be to defend the area where your current hook is instead of going to defend higher progress gens that are far away. This is especially the case if the survivor on hook is death hook.
- This is where good hook placement begins to come into play. Typically, if you can drag a survivor deep into a corner of the map in an area where there are 3 or 4 generators surrounding the hook, you can make it very difficult for survivors to work on any of the nearby gens and unhook the survivor without giving you various hits or downs in the process.  
- In this scenario, you can use about half your phase bar for travelling between/patrolling the gens as well as scouting for anyone coming in for the unhook, and then have your power recharged fully to follow up for quick hits and downs as the survivors either a) try to work on the gens around the hook or b) try to come into the area to unhook the survivor.
#### Agitation
- Using the perk Agitation will help confirm good hook placement almost every time. Aside from having a good initial hook, good hook placement is crucial in the mid to late game as well. It can be game ending for you if you’re able to drag a survivor and hook them:
- right on top of a high progressed generator that connects multiple 3 gens
- deep in a 3 or 4 gen when it gets down to the last 1 or 2 gens remaining
- between various progressed generators that are being actively worked so the hook is easily accessible to you as you pressure multiple gens and survivors
### Playing Efficiently
- I know that everyone has different mindsets when it comes to how you play Dead by Daylight, and some people don’t want to tunnel, camp, or slug. However, if you want to play Spirit the most efficiently, I am here to tell you that Spirit thrives when the game becomes a 3v1. If you’re playing as efficiently as possible, your objective as Spirit should always be to try to get someone out of the game as early as you can. Even if you don’t have a tight 3 or 4 gen once it gets down to the last 1 or 2 generators, Spirit will typically still be able to win if there are 3 survivors in the game instead of 4. She is very good at ‘snowballing’ when there are 3 survivors left – catching survivors out of position (not around strong resources), getting quick hits and downs, and catching injured survivors rotating to go heal or unhook/pick up downed survivors which can often lead to then, everyone either on hook or slugged on the ground.
- This doesn’t mean that it is always in your best interest to immediately tunnel one survivor out of the game, however. Given especially all of the anti-tunnel perks that currently exist (i.e., Off the Record, Decisive Strike, Shoulder the Burden, Babysitter, etc.), these perks can often allow a survivor to make a lot of distance outside of where you originally hooked them (and perhaps the area you want to protect) and waste a lot of your time in an area you don’t want to be in. If the survivor that you’re trying to tunnel makes a lot of distance towards a really safe area of the map that is away from the area you’re trying to defend, it is typically better to go for someone else that you know you’ll be able to hook in a more favorable place, and then a) tunnel this survivor instead (who might not have as many anti-tunnel perks available to them and/or won’t be able to make much distance once they’re unhooked), or b) potentially catch the survivor that you already hooked when they’re more out of position/ in a more favourable area. 
- Again, Agitation can be really good for assuring that the survivor will not be able to get much distance away from the area in which you hooked them, or worst case, they still die close enough that you can drag them and hook them again in a still relatively favorable spot.
- Bottom line, you should always try to prioritize getting a survivor out of the game as quick as possible, but don’t completely throw away your area defence for it. If you know that you have some time before crucial generators will become completed, then you can afford to commit away from the area for a potential tunnel out. This will really come down to how quick your chases can be and developing an ‘internal clock’ to know how much time you can afford to chase, and when you will need to drop/not commit a chase to go back to your area and pressure something else.
## Learning Mechanics and Tracking
### Mindset When Learning
- I know it can be a bit of a difficult mindset to get into, but try not to worry too much about winning while you're still learning spirit's mechanics. She's a very mechanically demanding killer and gets punished very harshly when you miss phases, so you will lose ALOT at first and that's okay!! Go into your matches with the mindset that you’re just trying to learn her mechanics in chase. Get used to all of the different noises in the game and start building your muscle memory a little bit each game. Then, once you do get the hang of tracking, you can start thinking about the other elements of the game.
- Along with this, I would highly recommend using a software such as OBS to record your games. When you’re learning Spirit, you have to spend a lot of time developing a muscle memory for all the different audio in this game, along with learning how to utilize all of the other aspects of her mechanics efficiently, before you can get to a point where you’re not getting steamrolled every game. Something that can really help you accelerate this process though is recording your games so that you can go back and see where you made mistakes or how you could’ve played chases differently. Of course, the main thing is to just practice a lot, but recording and rewatching your chases can help prevent you from creating bad habits or making the same mistakes in the future.
### Visual Cues
- While you’re learning how to recognize and identify the sounds in the game, there are other things you can look for to help your tracking as well. Although you can’t see survivors, auras, or survivor’s blood while phasing, there are a variety of visual ques that will occur in the game that you can see while phasing and can utilize to pinpoint where a survivor is. 
- Generator sparks: when a survivor is working on a generator, you can see sparks emitting from the specific side of the gen they’re working on. 
- Grass: When a survivor moves through patches of grass, you can see the grass moving in the place that they are. This is also the case for other map-specific environmental things such as corn on Coldwind, bushes on Dead Dog Saloon, or cattail plants on Swamp.
- Dust/Dirt: When a survivor vaults a dropped pallet, you can see dust that gets ‘kicked up’ on the side of the pallet that they vaulted from. Once you get more experience with Spirit, it’ll become a lot easier to identify what side they vaulted from just by the audio, but this can be a good way to help you at the beginning. 
- Footsteps: Some maps have visual footsteps that the survivor will leave behind on the ground. For instance, in the snow on Ormond Mine. 
- Hooks: When a survivor is on a hook, you can see the animation of the hook change if the survivor begins to get unhooked. 
### Phasing Tricks
Turning Sideways
- Something you’ll definitely encounter in chase with a survivor is Double-Backing. This is when a survivor is running in one direction but then they double back and start moving the way they came – this is typically to try to either make you waste more time in phase or trick you into coming out of phase too early so they’re able to make another resource. Survivors will often do this more than once or around corners/doorways to make it more difficult to pinpoint exactly where they are. A little trick to help with this is to turn your character model sideways and pause as you’re phasing - this will help you to be able to pinpoint the survivor faster since it’s easier to identify audio that is on the left or right side of you in comparison to something that is in front or behind. Even if you don’t yet suspect that a survivor has double-backed, a good habit to fall into is to just do quick microsecond pauses in phase and turn sideways in areas where you suspect a good survivor may double back. I will typically do this if I see the survivor attempting to break line of sight before I enter phase, when entering buildings or going around corners in phase, or if I see a change in scratch marks up ahead. Once you confirm that the survivor hasn’t double-backed, then you can continue your pursuit forward without wasting hardly any time.
#### When to Come Out of Phase
- Don’t be afraid to come out of phase if you can’t pinpoint where the survivor is! My rule of thumb typically is that if I can’t identify where the survivor exactly is after 2 seconds, I come out of phase. It’s much better to just come out early, get your power recharged faster, and reposition yourself to start phasing again. Rather then, hesitate too long in phase and give the survivor distance to stealth away or make another resource and then still have to wait for the majority or entirely of your power to be recharged. 
Chase Music
- Something you might struggle with when learning Spirit is tracking through the chase music. Although it only lingers for a few seconds after you begin phasing, sometimes it’s enough to throw you off, especially if the survivor is already close to you. If this is something that really bothers you, sometimes I will walk at a survivor backwards (or from an angle where I’m not looking directly at them) to prevent chase music from starting before I phase. For instance, I’ll do this if I’m playing on a map that already has loud ambient noises (such as, Ormond Mine, Toba Landing, or in the main room of Lery’s) and want the extra silence to track survivors.
#### ‘Shimmer Tech’
- If you’re ever searching for survivors across the map, you can utilize the ‘shimmer tech’ with your power by just tapping your m2 or power button—this will briefly begin the charge animation. Because a survivor disappears off your screen when you begin the charge animation, if any part of them is visible from a distance, they’ll stick out to you when you tap your power since you’ll notice that area of the screen change. 
#### Delaying Hits
- If you’re about to injure a survivor, have a few extra seconds before your power is fully recharged, and the survivor is not beside a resource, wait a couple seconds before injuring them so you can immediately follow up in phase to get the down quickly/potentially prevent them from making a resource. You should try to do this especially if you have chase perks like Rapid Brutality, so you can utilize them to their maximum potential in phase. 
#### Looking Down
- This obviously isn’t any kind of requirement, however, I always found it really helpful to look down while phasing—both when I was learning Spirit and even now. By looking down, you’re limiting what you look at visually on screen and are able to focus more on what you’re hearing. Of course, you still want to be looking up enough that you can react to environmental elements (such as, grass moving) but otherwise, this is definitely a nice trick to implement when learning Spirit. 
#### Falling off Heights
- If you know that you’ll need to fall from a great height in chase relatively soon and you already have your power charged (or it’s close to being ready), wait until you’re falling to start charging your power. If you charge your power at the top of the height and fall, you risk wasting precious seconds of your phase time airborne. Waiting until your mid-fall will allow you to have the entire duration of your phase on the ground – allowing you to make more distance.
- If you’re still waiting for your power to recharge when falling from a height, swing as you’re falling off the ledge. Doing this will cause your character to fall straight down instead of diagonally, it will also allow you to hit the ground faster as opposed to when falling normally. This will allow you to close some extra distance on the survivor you’re chasing while still waiting for your power. 
#### Base-kit Lunge
- When Spirit exits phase, her in-phase movement speed remains for a couple seconds pushing her in the direction of the way you’re facing. This means that Spirit technically has an extended lunge as she exiting phase if you swing as soon as you exit. You can utilize this to get hits if you’re just short of reaching the survivor in phase. Along with this, using addons (such as Mother-Daughter Ring, Muddy Sports Cap, or Zori) or perks to increase your movement speed in phase will extend this lunge even more!
- You can also utilize this to get your Spirit over windows and pallets if you swing out of phase from a higher elevation (for example, a window that is positioned beside a hill).
#### Dealing With Anti-Spirit Perks & Fogviles
- We all know that at this point in time, there are many perks that act as a direct counter to Spirit by making the survivor a lot more difficult to track. Some of these perks include, Iron Will, Light-footed, Lucky Break, Champion of Light, Off the Record, etc. Something very common that you’ll see a survivor with one of these perks (or a fogvile) do is instantly try to stealth you out once they’ve broke line of sight/you begin phasing. In this case, phase to where you last saw them and if you can’t instantly locate where they are, come out of phase. If you stay in phase for too long, all you’re doing is giving the survivor more distance to stealth away to another resource or even maybe to lose them completely. So yes, if you can’t instantly pinpoint their location, come out of phase, locate them again, and reposition yourself to phase again. 
#### Using Mother-Daughter Ring
- I would almost always recommend using double recharge addons while you’re learning tracking (Rusty Flute and Broken Watch), so that you can spend as much time in your power as possible with the minimum amount of punishment for missing phases. However, using Mother Daughter Ring while learning can provide many benefits too. Because MDR removes scratch marks in phase, you only have game audio to rely on – allowing you to build your muscle memory for audio a lot faster since you don’t have scratch marks even as a guideline.
- Along with this, using MDR will also help to discipline you faster with proper zoning and walking survivors down to a more secluded area before you begin phasing. This is one of the aspects of Spirit’s mechanics that you will need to learn after you’re confident in tracking, so that you can almost guarantee that every phase will result in a hit or down. In order to do this, you must limit the survivor’s potential pathways for when you’re in phase, or put them in a position where they can get hit no matter what they do - this includes, limiting 50/50 situations and the survivor’s ability to prestealth you in phase. With MDR, it is an absolute necessity for you to do this anyway. Otherwise, it will be very easy to get prestealthed since you don’t have scratch marks to tell you when or where the survivor stopped running. You must always put yourself in a position where you can phase to the survivors last known location or to an area where you know you’ll be able to locate some kind of survivor audio before they’re able to stealth effectively (i.e., gain enough distance that will completely hide all audio such as heavy and idle breathing, or have enough time to reset their heavy breathing). So yes, MDR can be very effective in helping with these elements of learning Spirit. However, I would highly recommend that once you’re confident in tracking, you take MDR off so that you don’t start to crutch on the strong movement speed it provides you. Otherwise, you will be hindered in your ability to learn other crucial elements of her power effectively, such as proper phase pathing and husk placement (as using this addon will result in you not being punished as much for making mistakes with these since you’re moving so fast).
## Mindgaming
- As I will thoroughly go over in the Husk Placement and Phase Pathing at Every Tile section, you want to try to put yourself in as many scenarios as possible where you can practically guarantee yourself the hit. You do this by, as I’ll describe, making it a habit to walk the survivor down enough to have good husk placement (husk placement that will either block a pallet or window or zone the survivor from pathing away to another resource) as well as good phase pathing (pathing that will utilize the least amount of your power and/or put yourself in a position to always be able to react to whatever the survivor chooses to do). With that being said, however, you’re going to find yourself in scenarios where you’ll have to mind game with your phase. Sometimes you don’t have the luxury of walking a survivor down as much as you’d want to, as doing so would allow them to make a strong area of the map. 
### What is Mindgaming with Phase?
- Because the survivor can’t see Spirit and only has the directional audio of your phase to rely on, you can utilize this to make the survivor you’re chasing think that you’re committing one side of a tile or filler and bait them to either a) drop/vault a pallet, b) vault a window, or c) leave a resource. Once this happens, you can then quickly double back around the way you came or follow up and hit them without giving them time to react. 
- As I’ll get into thoroughly in the Husk Placement and Phasing Pathing at Every Tile section, your first priority should always be to put yourself in a position that will guarantee the hit on the survivor. However, sometimes this is out of your control (such as, if you have to start phasing from a distance to prevent the survivor from making a very strong resource) and you might have to rely on mindgaming at a filler pallet to get the hit so they can’t get the distance they need to make that stronger resource. 
### Specialty Kinds of Spirit Mindgames
#### Basement/Shack Pallet Mindgame
- See Killer Shack, No Pallet Control, Scenario 1: Enter from Top of Shack from Section 4 of the Guide. This is an example scenario where you don’t have any other choice but to mindgame, otherwise you’ll be forced to break a pallet.
- Something else to note though is you can actually preform this kind of mindgame on almost any strong pallet or window that’s next to a longer wall. The idea overall is to use the directional audio to your advantage by making the survivor unsure of what side of a wall you’re on. For example, hugging a wall in phase to make the survivor think you’re on the other side of it and baiting them to vault back into you. Another example of where this is effective is with windows on different elevations – phasing towards a window underneath of it to make the survivor think you’re phasing to the vault and baiting them to vault and fall into you.
#### Phase Animation Mindgame
- Spirit has a very small visual queue from the survivors POV that tells them when she has started phasing. A lot of survivors don’t actually know that this visual queue exists, but experienced survivors will, so you can sometimes utilize this to bait a survivor to vault into you early if they know that all you have to do is short phase to hit them. 
- This mind game isn’t very optimal, as you can typically just position your husk correctly at a pallet or window and then if the survivor vaults into you, they’re bodyblocked in the resource anyway and you’re guaranteed the hit, but it’s still a cool thing to know about. See How to Block Pallets and Windows.
#### Stand Still Mindgame
- Like I said for the Phase Animation Mindgame, a lot of survivors don’t think there is a visual queue for when you start phasing and even if you do know about it, it’s very difficult to see. With that being said, if they think you’re going to short phase, they’ll often want to prevault a pallet or window. So sometimes you can stand still for a moment, doing absolutely nothing, and have the survivor vault into your loving arms.
#### Faking Phase Direction
- When Spirit begins phasing, her husk will always face in the direction that she started phasing in and stay in that position/facing that direction until she comes out of phase. You can utilize this to trick survivors that you’re going to phase in the direction that you’re facing, and then phase somewhere else instead. 
- For instance, if you’re chasing an injured survivor near another survivor who is working on a generator, you can begin phasing facing the direction of the injured survivor to lure the survivor that’s working on the generator into a false sense of security that you’re going to phase after and down the injured survivor. Instead, however, you’ll quickly phase to the survivor on the generator. If the survivor doesn’t react quickly, this can often result in you grabbing them off of the generator. This also works for survivors on totems, gates, chests, or anything that locks them into an animation.
- I also like using this mindgame if I suspect that a survivor is stealthing. For instance, if I can’t find a survivor hiding around a progressed generator, I’ll fake as if I’m phasing away from the generator to go check on another one that has progress. This will often bait the survivor out of hiding and you can catch them by the gen. 
- This also applies to survivors that you do see stealthing somewhere. By facing another direction as you begin phasing, you increase the likelihood of baiting them into thinking you haven’t seen them so they remain in the same hiding place. This way, by the time you phase to them, they won’t have enough time or distance to actually make a resource.

# How to Block Pallets and Windows
### Dropped Pallets
- For those who didn’t already know, Spirit’s husk has collision, which means not only can she block Doorways and Undropped Pallets, but she can actually body block a survivor between her husk and the dropped pallet if the survivor vaults the pallet when her husk is set up there.
#### Placement for Every Pallet. 
- There are a lot of pallets around the maps that will have parts of the tile stick out to help make body blocking the pallet a lot easier. However, you should try to get into the habit of using the correct placement of your husk so that you don’t accidentally mess it up on a pallet that doesn’t have anything else to help you. This is the placement to properly block a dropped pallet. 
- If you place your husk right against the pallet/too close, the survivor will vault through you.
- If you place your husk too far away, the survivor will be able to go around your husk and move away after they vault.

### Overall Ranks (Based on Gameplay & Audio)
#### Worst Maps
##### Erie of Crows (Foresaken Boneyard)
- Erie of Crows is by far Spirit’s worst map to play on for both, gameplay and audio.
###### Map Size, Gen Spread, & Playstyle
- An average sized map with horrible gen spreads:
- Even though this map is pretty averagely sized, the generators are very spread out, making the map feel a lot larger than it is. As we’ve discussed, Spirit thrives in protecting an area with close generators, and the generators on Erie of Crows are typically very far apart. You might get lucky and have a tighter 3-gen spawn around Shack or the right-side of Main Building, but this doesn’t happen often. 
- This means that you typically have to use a lot of, if not all of your power to traverse between the generators, which means you can’t follow up in chase with your power right away and the survivor will be able to make it to a safe resource.
- Your best bet to winning on this map is making sure you guard the area where your gens are the closest right from the beginning of the game. If the survivors complete one of those generators early on, leaving your generators very spread apart (even more than they already are), you’ll most likely lose. So, try to avoid this!!
- This means be careful where you decide to take chase. If the area you want to defend is around Shack, the survivors are actively trying to work those gens, and the survivor has a lot of distance to run down the map to Main Building, it’s not worth pursuing a chase there unless the survivor is death hook and injured. A down on the wrong side of the map is not worth losing an important generator for Spirit.
###### Resources
- Aside from Badham Preschool, this map is the worst in terms of strong and connectable resources. There are safe fillers everywhere on the map that easily connect to strong buildings such as Killer Shack and Main Building. 
- A lot of these resources are also right in the center of the map, making them very easily accessible.
- This means that there are a lot of strong resources surrounding generators as well, making it a lot more difficult to catch survivors off guard or catch them when leaving a generator. It also allows them to commit generators longer since they don’t have to pre run—something you typically want to do against Spirit so you don’t accidentally go down near generators the Spirit will want to defend.
###### Audio Recap
- Footstep noises on this map are the most difficult to hear of any map that exists in the game due to the Sand floor audio. This is especially evident if the survivor is using a cosmetic with quieter footstep audio (such as boots or sandals). The only places where this does not apply is where there is no Sand flooring (Main Building second floor on Erie of Crows, Shack, etc.)
- An ‘average’ sized map with horrible gen spreads:
- Even though this map is pretty averagely sized, the generators are very spread out, making the map feel a lot larger than it is. As we’ve discussed, Spirit thrives in protecting an area with close generators, and the generators on Hawkins are typically very far apart. Even if you appear to have a tighter 3-gen, the wall RNG will often place long solid walls between them, making it take 10x longer to traverse between the generators and the survivors working on them will have a much bigger head start to pre leave as they’ll know you’re on your way from the other side of the wall.
- This means that you typically have to use a lot of, if not all of your power to traverse between the generators, which means you can’t follow up in chase with your power right away and the survivor will have been able to pre leave and make a safe resource.
- Don’t worry about trying to protect either the Portal Room generator or the second-floor generator (unless you somehow have a 3 gen around it). These two gens are typically a lot farther from the other gens and are very safe for the survivors to work on. 
- The last thing you want to see when it gets down to 1 or 2 gens left, is those 2 generators still uncompleted, so make sure to protect an area of generators on the first floor to the best of your ability (and pray you don’t get horrible wall RNG).
- This is one of the few maps where I highly recommend getting rid of resources, especially in the area you’re trying to defend. As we know, Spirit is one of the few killers that is good at playing around dropped pallets, but on this map, it is not the case. Because the pallets are so close together and so safe when chained together, you’ll be forced to play 50/50s with the survivor in many areas of this map. So, break some of these resources, and then play around the dropped pallets that are now isolated.

## Builds For Beginners
### Using Teachable Perks:
#### Corrupt Intervention, Scourge Hook: Pain Resonance, Sloppy Butcher, No Way Out
##### Explanation:
This is what I believe to be the BEST beginner Spirit build, although this build is exceptionally good for intermediate and experienced spirit players too.
- Corrupt Intervention: This serves to slow down the gens at the beginning of the game and give you extra time for your first chase. This is also great if you want to practice spreading pressure and go for multiple hits on survivors that are in the same area.
- Scourge Hook: Pain Resonance: This is great for an easy means of gen regression.
- Sloppy Butcher: This is so the survivors spend more time healing and off of gens. This will also incentivize the survivors to be injured more often which will be easier for your chases. 
- I think you could make the argument to replace Sloppy Butcher with a) some kind of information perk, such as Barbeque and Chili, or b) another gen regression/blocking perk, such as Grim Embrace. These two perks will also serve an effective purpose of helping you either a) save time in knowing where to go after hooking (for Barbeque and Chili), or b) have extra time for chases after hooking (for Grim Embrace) while you’re learning. However, I personally like Sloppy Butcher the most here.
- And No Way Out: This perk is great alongside Pain Resonance as you’ll be collecting those stacks anyway, and it’ll give you even more time by slowing down end game for you. I think as you become a more experienced spirit player very rarely will you ever make it to endgame, but while you’re still learning tracking, it’s a great cushion. 
The goal of these perks is to slow down the game as much as possible without the player having to do much to activate them. This way they can focus all their time on chasing and practicing their tracking with spirit as opposed to making many game decisions.
If you’d like to see this build put into action, you can watch this video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w8X28_yib70&t=81s&ab_channel=Remylea
### Using Non-Teachable Perks:
#### No Holds Barred (Deadlock), Sloppy Butcher, Spies from the Shadows, No One Escapes Death
##### Explanation:
This is what I believe to be the BEST beginner Spirit build using non-teachable perks, although this build is exceptionally good for intermediate and experienced Spirit players too.
- No Holds Barred (Deadlock): Because Pinhead’s perks are become general/non-teachable perks now, this means Deadlock will be free to use. This is the only gen-block perk that exists in the game that is also a non-teachable so it’s definitely worth running to slow down gen progress for you.
- Sloppy Butcher: This is so the survivors spend more time healing and off of gens. This will also incentivize the survivors to be injured more often which will be easier for your chases. This is an incredibly strong perk on Spirit, hence why it’s also in my Using Teachable Perks build.
- Spies from the Shadows: Definitely the best information perk available to you from the pool of non-teachables. This is a great option for information even if you have all of the teachables unlocked. I will say that it can be slightly overstimulating for Spirit with all of the noise notifications but it’s definitely still very good.
- No One Escapes Death: Much like the purpose of No Way Out in the Using Teachable Perks build, this perk is great for giving you some extra leeway in the endgame if it comes down to it. No Ed is very good at getting you some quick extra downs in endgame and will often lead to you being able to snowball if the survivors try to cleanse No Ed to save their teammate(s). Again, I think as you become a more experienced spirit player very rarely will you ever make it to endgame, but while you’re still learning tracking, it’s a great cushion. 
Again, the goal of these perks is to slow down the game as much as possible without the player having to do much to activate them. This way they can focus all their time on chasing and practicing their tracking with Spirit as opposed to making many game decisions. Although these perks, of course, aren’t as strong as in the Using Teachable Perks build, they are still really nice in providing you with some good gen slowdown, heal slowdown, information, and assistance in end game. 
## Hex Builds
I wanted to make a separate category for the best Hex Builds because while I don’t think they’re as strong as a Full Gen regression build, Spirit utilizes them incredibly well and can make games very miserable for survivors. Here are a few of my favourites:
#### Hex: Nothing But Misery, Hex: Blood Favor, Hex: Haunted Ground, Dominance
##### Explanation:
- Blood favor and Nothing but Misery: On their own, these two perks are already incredibly strong on Spirit, but they become even more lethal when used together. Not only will you hit the survivor and have all pallets within a 32-meter radius block for 15 seconds, but the survivor will also be 5% hindered for 15 seconds assuring that they won’t ever make it outside of the Blood Favor radius. In order for this to happen though, you need to keep Blood Favor alive until you can get all 8 stacks to activate Nothing by Misery. 
- Haunted Ground and Dominance: These two perks then become the other two crucial parts of the build to protect Blood Favor in the early game while you collect your Nothing but Misery stacks. Dominance will give you great information for knowing when a survivor has found one of your totems. This way you can defend it after it gets unblocked and potentially hook someone near it. Even if they do end of cleansing one of your totems, however, since Haunted Ground spawns two totems and the Nothing but Misery totem doesn’t spawn until it becomes activated, that means the survivor has a 66.6% chance of cleansing a Haunted Ground totem rather than your Blood Favor totem. 
- Haunted Ground is also just an incredibly strong perk for Spirit on its own. I can’t count how many times I’ve found myself in a scenario where I have someone on hook and am about to chase someone else when Haunted Ground gets cleansed (making everyone exposed), leading to a quick down on the person I’m chasing and am now in a position to end the game if even one of the two other survivors give away their position. It’s such a strong snowball perk and can allow you to create so much pressure so easily.
#### Hex: Devour Hope, Hex: Face the Darkness, Hex: Thrill of the Hunt, Hex: Undying
##### Explanation:
The goal of this build is to assure that Devour Hope never gets cleansed. 
- Devour Hope: Spirit is one of the best users of Devour Hope. She can get to 3 stacks very quickly given how quick her chases can be on their own, and once the survivors are exposed on the 3rd stack, you can end the game very quickly. Having the survivors exposed makes her chase 10x more lethal to what it already was, and it’ll be easy to catch them out of position/on rotations while they’re a) trying to unhook their teammates/pick their teammates up off the ground, or b) looking for your totems. You only need 5 stacks, 5 hooks, before survivors become moriable which Spirit can get very quickly and a lot of the time survivors won’t be able to keep up. And even if they are somehow able to cleanse all of your totems, by this point you should have so much pressure that it won’t even matter in a lot of cases.
- Thrill of the Hunt: At the beginning of the match, you’ll have all 5 stacks of Thrill of the Hunt, meaning that it will take 60% longer for survivors to cleanse your totems. This will make it super hard for survivors to cleanse them as it’ll give you extra time to pressure them off of the totem and it’ll waste so much more of their time each time they attempt it.
- Face the Darkness: This is the next crucial part of this build. Along with Thrill of the Hunt, which with 5 stacks will make totem cleansing take over 30 seconds, it’ll be near impossible for survivors to cleanse your totems as Face the Darkness will cause them to scream in incriminates of 25 seconds when they’re outside of your terror radius (and keep in mind, Spirit doesn’t have a terror radius when she’s phasing so survivors are bound to scream more often than if you used it on other killers).
- Just remember you do have to activate this totem yourself by hitting a survivor and leaving them injured, and will keep having to activate it if that survivor ever heals or gets put into the dying state. So just make sure you keep track of it to keep it activated. 
- Undying: Undying is the last part of this build that is necessary for keeping Devour alive (uncleansed). It gives you a bit of leeway to allow one of your totems to get cleansed because, after all, it’s difficult to keep survivors off of 4 totems on your own. Say, for instance, there’s a few moments where Face the Darkness hasn’t been activated yet and a survivor gets right on your totem, Undying provides a bit of cushioning to make sure whatever is cleansed will not be Devour. 
#### Hex: Ruin, Hex: Wretched Fate, Hex: Blood Favour, Hex: Pentimento
##### Explanation:
This build includes some of the strongest Hex perks on Spirit, plus Pentimento to punish the survivors for cleansing your totems (because they’re definitely going to want to).
- Ruin: Ruin is a very strong gen regression perk for Spirit. Because she can injure survivors so quickly and easily, having ruin will often put survivors in a situation where they have to choose between going to heal and allowing their gen to regress or working on the gen injured. If they’re smart, they’ll go heal and then you have free gen regression. If they keep working on the gen injured, you can down them near the gen and either a) hook them near it and the gen will regress anyway since the other survivors won’t be able to work on it safely or b) slug the survivor/snowball and the gen will regress anyway too. No matter which they choose to do, you’re highly benefiting either way.
- Wretched Fate: This perk doesn’t activate until 1 generator is completed, but once that happens, the obsession is forced to go search for their totem unless they want to work gens 33% slower. This means one less survivor working gens while you’re pressuring other survivors, and you can typically catch the obsession on rotation looking for their totem pretty easily. 
- Blood Favour: Blood Favour is one of Spirit’s strongest Hex totems and chase perks. If you have your power mostly or fully recharged when you injure a survivor, they won’t be able to make it outside of Blood Favor’s 32-meter radius before you phase on top of them again.
- Pentimento: Again, Pentimento is then crucial for this build to punish the survivors for cleansing your totems (because they’re definitely going to want to) and waste even more of their time by having to go and try to cleanse the Pentimento totems after they’re lit as well. 
## Strongest Builds
### Full Gen Regression Build
#### Corrupt Intervention, Scourge Hook: Pain Resonance, Pop Goes the Weasel, Eruption 
##### Explanation:
- Pain Resonance: Pain Resonance is one of, if not the best gen regression perk in the game. It’s easy to use since it’ll always be accessible to you so long as you have a scourge hook nearby. Even if you decide to tunnel and only use one stack in your first 3 hooks, it still slows down the gen progress a lot for the other 3 survivors. Then, once it becomes a 3v1, you still have 3 of your stacks to use.
- Eruption: Eruption is the next crucial part of this build. As I discussed in Playstyle, Spirit is incredibly strong at defending an area of a map (a 3 or 4 gen) and eruption is one of the best tools to help protect those gens. Because Spirit is so good at getting quick downs when she needs to, she is one of the best users of Eruption.
- Remember that this perk has an aura reading element to it. Survivors will often forget that you see their aura after they’re hit with eruption, so make sure to take advantage of this and watch where they path to before starting phase.
- Pop Goes the Weasel: I wouldn’t recommend using this perk on its own, but paired with Eruption, Pop becomes very strong on Spirit. It’s good because you get to use it after every hook and since you’ll be kicking gens anyway for Eruption, it makes for a really nice combo with it just to give you that extra time for downing another survivor and making sure that gen gets hit by eruption. 
### Full Chase Build
#### Hex: Blood Favour, Hex: Undying, Save the Best for Last, Rapid Brutality
##### Explanation:
It’s easy to think that an all-gen slowdown build would be the strongest build for Spirit, and you’re probably right. However, Spirit is one of the strongest killers in the game when it comes to her lethality in chase. This build, in my opinion, is almost equally as strong as a full gen regression build because it allows her chases to be so fast that the survivors simply can’t keep up with the amount of pressure you build. 
- Rapid Brutality and Save the Best for Last: This perk combination alone is one of Spirit’s best. Having 8 stacks of Save the Best for Last is lethal on its own, but pairing this with Rapid Brutality so you can recover insanely quickly AND have haste to either a) walk down the survivor while you’re waiting for your power to recharge or b) phase with extra movement speed, leaves very little options for the survivor you’re chasing unless they happen to be right beside a pallet…
- Blood Favour: Which is why this build then has Blood Favor. As I explained in one of the Hex Builds, Blood Favour is one of Spirit’s strongest Hex totems and chase perks. If you have your power mostly or fully recharged when you injure a survivor, they won’t be able to make it outside of Blood Favor’s 32-meter radius before you phase on top of them again. So, pairing this with Rapid Brutality and Save the Best for Last becomes incredibly lethal because when you injure a survivor, you’ll not only recover insanely fast and have Haste for 10 seconds, but the pallets within a 32-metter range will also be blocked. 
- Undying: Undying is then important here just to make sure Blood Favor stays active for as long as possible. For example, there’s not much you can do if a survivor just happens to spawn on one of your totems, so having Undying will always give it another chance no matter what and then you can defend that one totem when it gets down to it. 
## Best Addons
This is a screenshot of how I would rank each Spirit addon. The main points you want to take away from using her addons are:
SSS/BEST 
- Rusty Flute (40% recharge)
- Okay so getting right into it, starting at the very top of the strongest addon category, Rusty Flute is by far Spirit’s best addon due to how valuable and almost crucial recharge speed is on Spirit. In order to understand this a little better, lets’ talk a bit about Spirit’s power in general. Overall, Spirit has one of the most punishing powers of any killer in the entire game. If you misuse her power, meaning you use it without getting a hit or down from it, you then have to walk down the survivor as a 110 killer with no power for anywhere up to 15 seconds. And before you try to make the argument, “well Rem that’s what her passive phasing is for”, No, her passive phasing is not something that you can consistently rely on at all to get hits. Not only is it something that you can’t see and have zero control over, but Spirit’s breathing is so loud that the survivor should almost always be able to pinpoint where you are whether you’re passive phasing or not. Spirit’s passive phasing is also unlike Onryos in the sense that it doesn’t make her go entirely invisible for multiple seconds, so there are always visual queues to tell where she is. With that being said, the survivor should almost never get hit by you during this period of downtime between phases unless they have no resources or you’re at such a shitty pallet that you’d probably be able to get the hit even without passive phasing. This means youre only option during this downtime typically is to wwaste time waiting for your power to come back and walk down the survivor to the best of your ability. I also want to highlight though, that even if you use her power correctly and successfully hit every phase, the downtime between being able to use your power by dafeult is way too long. The survivor should almost always make cross map distance off their initial hit if you use most or even half of your phase bar to get the hit. 
- With that being said, return back to what I said about Rusty flute being a necessary or crucial addon. Rusty Flute provides a 40% increase to recharge speed, allowing you to heavily close that gap between getting the hit and being able to use your power again – meaning you can down the survivor without them making cross map distance. It also makes Spirit’s power much more forgiving for missed phased or learning Spirit players who are bound to continuously misuse her power – obviously, because they’re learning. Even for experienced spirit players, the audio in this game is far from perfect and perks like iron will, lightfooted, lucky break, etc. exist, so sometimes you’ll lose the survivor in phase and will be forced to come out. If you come out relatively quickly, Rusty Flute then allows you to quickly readjust your positioning and phase again without losing too much distance on the survivor. 
- Overall, without recharge speed, Spirit’s power is 10x more punishing than it already is, and makes Spirit a significantly weaker killer than she is with recharge speed. It is quite literally the difference of her being an S tier killer and a low A tier killer (I know her position on tier lists is debatable, but regardless, you get my point). 
- Mother Daughter Ring (25% speed)
- Following Rusty Flute, Spirit’s next best addon is MDR. MDR has undergone many nerfs up until this point – on release it offered a 66% increase to Spirit’s movement speed in phase, then it was nerfed to 40%, and now it sits at 25%. While MDR is obviously much weaker than what it used to be, it is still an incredibly strong addon that makes Spirit 10x stronger than she is by default. Spirit already has, in my opinion, the second strongest chase power in the entire game next to nurse, and having this kind of speed basically takes away any kind of counterplay that the survivor might have simply because they cannot react to what they’re hearing at all. For those who don’t know, there is a couple second delay from the time Spirit begins phasing to the time that the survivor can actually pinpoint where the directional audio is, so if Spirit is anywhere near you and is using MDR, she will already be on top of your head by the time you can hear where she is. 
- Along with the insane chase potential it offers for movement speed, MDR is also incredibly strong due to the suedo recharge speed that it provides. Because you’re moving so fast, you won’t need to use as much of your power to get the injury or down as you typically would, meaning your power has less to recharge so you get your power back faster. This is something to keep in mind for all of Spirit’s movement speed addons – Speed basically also equals recharge speed, but it is specifically the case for MDR because of how significant the speed increase is. 
- It’s also significant to note the benefits that MDR provides in terms of map pressure. Spirit typically has horrible map pressure since she cannot use her phase to traverse the map and then instantly use it in chase, meaning the most she can do is 1) use a bit of her power for traversing so she has it readily available or OR 2) walk around the map as a 110 killer to then instantly use her power where she needs to. This obviously doesn’t offer very good map pressure in comparison to killers like blight, billy, ghoul, etc., which is why Spirit typically thrives in area defence, playing around one side of a map, and holding tight 3 or 4 gens. Using MDR, however, gives her much more flexibility for these things that she would not have using any other addon.
- Lastly, I also just want to quickly highlight the benefits that this addon offers to learning Spirit players. MDR makes it so you don’t have scratch marks in phase – this is the trade-off or “downside” that was given to the addon for having really high movement speed. This obviously makes it difficult to use this addon if you aren’t already experienced with tracking audio. However, I feel like this can actually be really good for quickly disciplining a newer spirit player who is still learning tracking by having to rely strictly on audio alone as opposed to having all of the other visual elements of the game.
- Rin’s Broken Watch (30% recharge)
- Next, I’m placing Rin’s Broken Watch in this category basically for the same reasons as Rusty Flute. Broken Watch is 30% recharge speed as opposed to Rusty Flute’s 40%, and while it’s a sizeable difference, it’s still a very strong addon basically for all of the same reasons – so just to recap, this recharge speed allows you to 1) heavily close that gap between getting the hit and being able to use your power again – meaning you can down the survivor without them making cross map distance, 2) makes her power much more forgiving for missed phased or learning Spirit players, as well as 3) come out of phase, quickly readjust your positioning, and phase again without losing too much distance on the survivor. 
- Muddy Sports Day Cap (10% speed)
- The final addon I’m placing in this category is Muddy Sports Day Cap. Obviously this addon doesn’t have nearly the same extent of benefits that MDR offers, since the movement speed comparison is 25% to 10%. However, it is still by far her best addon aside from the three previously mentioned. # Husk Placement and Phase Pathing at Every Tile
In all of my experience and observations of other Spirit players over the years, I’ve learned that there are two types that exist. The first are Spirit players that simply rely on good tracking abilities. They see a survivor and try to phase on top of them, without really thinking about other mechanics like husk placement, zoning potential, and phase pathing. The second are Spirit players who incorporate the latter. They consider every mechanical element that Spirit has available to her and THEN phase on top of the survivor. Spirit is a strong enough killer in chase that you can get away with being either type of player most of the time if your tracking abilities/phase skills are already very good. You will, however, find yourself getting punished for it in chase against good survivors who know how to play against Spirit. 
For example, if you are the first type of Spirit player, I’m sure you often find yourself relying on winning 50/50s at a pallet or window to get a hit. You probably end up winning them a lot of the time, but regardless, it is still a 50/50 that the survivor has the chance of winning. If you are the second player, however, and incorporate those other mechanics along with your good tracking, those 50/50s become 90/10s in your favor at the majority of tiles and fillers. 
In this section of the Spirit Guide, I will go over every unique tile that exists in Dead by Daylight as of June 2025, provide various possible scenarios that you could find yourself in when chasing a survivor there, and show you a) where to position your husk, b) where your “pause point” should be, and c)  how you should then path with your phase to guarantee the hit nearly every time.
## Key
Red Dot = Husk/Where You Should Begin Your Phase
Red Arrows = The Most Efficient Phase Pathing to The Pause Point
White Dot = Pause Point

## Pallet Gyms
### Pallet Control
#### Scenario 1: Survivor on Longwall
- If you chase a survivor into a Pallet Gym, they give you pallet control (i.e., greed the pallet) and move towards the long side of the tile, begin by setting up your husk to properly block the pallet. See How to Block Pallets.
- Phase to the pause point and listen. The survivor will either:
- Stand still.
- Move towards the now blocked pallet.
- Leave the tile.
- Remember: It’s important to be patient and listen to where the survivor is and what they’re doing before coming out of phase. If you’re husk is properly blocking a pallet and you choose to short phase to where you think the survivor is, rather than be patient and listen, you run the risk of them making it back to the pallet without getting the hit. 
- Once you’ve identified what they survivor is doing from the pause point, follow up in phase, then come out and hit them.
#### Scenario 2: Survivor on Short Wall
- If you chase a survivor into a Pallet Gym, they give you pallet control (i.e., greed the pallet) and move towards the short side of the tile, begin by properly blocking the pallet with your husk. See How to Block Pallets.
- Stand still.
- Leave the tile.
### No Pallet Control
- If you do not have pallet control, walk down the survivor and force them to drop the pallet without getting stunned. 
- If the survivor chooses to greed the pallet (not drop it), refer to Pallet Control, Pallet Not Dropped.
- Refer to Pallet Dropped.
### Pallet Dropped
- If the survivor is on the opposite side of the dropped pallet, set up your husk to block the dropped pallet. See How to Block Pallets. 
- Phase around the short side of the pallet gym to the pause point and listen. This will not only limit their options and time to react, but save you time as well. The survivor will either:
- Stand still.
- Vault the pallet, becoming stuck between your husk and the pallet.
- Move towards you.
- Move along the long side of the pallet gym.
- Leave the tile.
- Once you’ve identified what the survivor is doing from the pause point, follow up in phase, then come out and hit them.
## Short Wall Jungle Gyms
### Pallet Control
#### Scenario 1: Survivor Outside of Tile/Vaulted Window 
- If you have pallet control at this tile and the survivor is on the outside (I.e. the other side of the window), begin by setting up your husk to block the pallet. See How to Block Pallets. 
- Stand still.
- Vault the window.
- Move towards you, attempting to make it back into the tile or the now blocked pallet.
- Move along the long wall.
- Leave the tile.
#### Scenario:2 Survivor Inside of Tile
- If you have pallet control at this tile and the survivor is on the inside (I.e. the opposite wall from the pallet), begin by setting up your husk to block the pallet. 
- Stand still.
- Move towards you (i.e., towards the window, or further inside the tile).
- Move around the wall towards the window.
- Leave the tile.
#### Scenario 3: Survivor Inside of Tile, Filler Close By
- Remember to always be conscientious of what else is around tiles. If, for example, there is a strong filler pallet beside this tile (on the right-side of this image), you will want to phase around the outside of the tile instead of inside to zone the survivor away from the filler. 
- Stand still.
- Move towards you and the now blocked pallet.
- Move towards the window.
- Move down the long wall.
- Leave the tile and try to chain to the filler pallet.
- Taking this phase route to ensure the survivor cannot make the close by filler means that the survivor will be able to get the fast vault on the window inside the tile. Don’t be afraid to linger at the pause point a little longer here to see what they decide to do. If you come out of phase too early and they vault the window, you’ll have to wait for recharge and repeat this whole process.
##### Example A: Survivor Doesn’t Vault
- In Example A, the survivor runs toward the window but doesn’t vault it (i.e., they’re trying to mind game you into thinking they’re taking the window so you either a) give them distance by wrapping around the wall too early or b) come out of phase too early allowing them to make it back to the pallet). Instead, however, you hover in the pause point, listen to them doing this, and simply follow up to hit them. 
##### Example B: Survivor Does Vault
- In Example B, the survivor runs toward the window and does fast vault it. If they do choose to instantly vault this window, simply wrap around the long side to the second pause point. Once you’ve listened and confirmed what the survivor is doing, come out of phase and hit them. They will not be able to successfully vault the window back at this point without you hitting them. 
### No Pallet Control
- If the survivor chooses to greed the pallet (not drop it), refer to Pallet Control, Scenario 1 or Pallet Control, Scenario 2.
- Refer to Pallet Dropped, Scenario 1 or Pallet Dropped, Scenario 2.
### Pallet Dropped
#### Scenario 1: Survivor on Inside of Tile
- If you are on the outside of the tile and the pallet is dropped, begin by setting up your husk to block the pallet. See How to Block Pallets.
- Phase around the short wall to the pause point and listen. The survivor will either 
- Stand still.
- Vault into your husk, becoming stuck between your husk and the pallet.
- Move towards you.
#### Scenario 2: Survivor on Outside of Tile
- If you are on the inside of the tile and the pallet is dropped, begin by setting up your husk to block the pallet. See How to Block Pallets. 
- Stand still.
- Move around the short side of the wall.
- Move along the long side of the wall.
- Leave the tile 
## Long Wall Jungle Gyms
### Pallet Control
- If you chase the survivor into the tile and have pallet control (i.e., they greeded the pallet), but the survivor does NOT immediately vault the window, begin by setting up your husk to block the pallet. See How to Block Pallets.
- Stand still.
- Vault the window.
- Move to the left.
- Move to the right.
- If the survivor does a), c), d), or e), follow up from the pause point in phase, then come out and hit them. If the survivor does b) and medium or slow vaults the window, you can also come out and hit them. If the survivor does b) and fast vaults the window before you can get within hit range, however, stay in phase and move to the second pause point:
- The survivor will either
- Stand still beside the window
- Medium or slow vault back.
- Move towards you.
- Move away from you and the window, trying to wrap around the tile.
- Leave the tile.
- Once you’ve identified what the survivor is doing from the second pause point, follow up in phase, then come out and hit them. 
- Even if the survivor decides to immediately medium vault back or run slightly away from the window to get a fast vault back, you will always be able to hit them before they can make it back through the vault (so long as you take this phase pathing).
- If the survivor gives you pallet control and is standing on the outside of the tile (i.e. they cannot get a fast vault on the window from their current position), begin by setting up your husk to block the pallet. You can essentially treat this scenario the same as a Pallet Gym (refer to section on Pallet Gyms) since the survivor shouldn’t be able to get the fast vault on the window before you reach them.
- Phase along the wall to the pause point and listen. The survivor will either:
- Stand still.
- Move around the outside of the tile towards the window.
- Move further into the tile.
- Leave the tile.
### No Pallet Control
### Pallet Dropped
- If the survivor is on the inside of the tile with the dropped pallet, begin by setting up your husk to block the pallet. See How to Block Pallets.
- Stand still.
- Move toward the window (and most likely vault it).
- You want to try to avoid this scenario. Always try to zone the survivor back into the tile before phasing so that you can be positioned on the outside, not them. See No Pallet Control, Scenario 1. This is because in this scenario, the dropped pallet is pretty safe for the survivor if played correctly. I.e., if they drop/vault the pallet and immediately wrap around the tile or are able to chain it to another close filler or tile, you do not want to be stuck on the inside as it’ll give the survivor unnecessary distance and potentially waste your power. 
- If you must play out this scenario however, and the survivor is on the outside of the tile with the dropped pallet, begin by setting up your husk to block the pallet. See How to Block Pallets.
- Stand still.
- Wrap around the tile.
- Leave the tile.
## TL Walls
### Scenario 1: Survivor Vaults T Wall
#### Example A: Enters from Left Side
- If you chase a survivor to a TL Wall that allows them to fast vault the T Wall, walk to the end of the T Wall and position your husk there.
- Phase to the pause point (i.e., the same corner of the T Wall that the survivor vaulted toward). The survivor will either:
- Stand still.
- Move back toward the vault to vault it again.
- Move towards you.
- Wrap around the long wall.
##### If There’s a Connecting Tile/Filler Above 
- Remember to always be conscientious of what else is around tiles. Because of the direction you’re chasing the survivor in this scenario, it may be easy for them to chain this window to another pallet or window close by. 
- For instance, if you chase the survivor into the tile from the left side and there’s a filler pallet above this tile, you’ll want to change your husk placement to zone them away from the filler or cut them off before they can reach it safely. 
- From here the survivor will either:
- Stand still.
- Vault the window back. 
- Move away from you towards the center of the tile.
- Move towards the connecting filler (and you).
- If the survivor does a), b), or d) then once you’ve identified what the survivor is doing from the pause point, follow up in phase, then come out and hit them. 
- If the survivor does c), however, follow up to the second pause point and listen.
- The survivor will either:
- Stand still.
- Double back from the way they came.
- Move towards the other window.
- Move into the center of the tile.
- Wrap around the outside of the tile.
- This pathing takes longer and uses more of your phase, but is necessary if you have to zone a survivor away from a stronger resource nearby. Once you’ve identified what the survivor is doing from the pause point, follow up in phase, then come out and hit them.
#### Example B: Enters from Right Side
- Stand still.
- Move towards you.
- For instance, if you chase the survivor into the tile from the right side and there’s a filler pallet above this tile, you’ll want to change your husk placement to zone them away from the filler or cut them off before they can reach it safely. 
- Stand still.
- Vault the window back. 
- Even if the survivor has enough distance to move towards the other window and vault it, you can react to their actions from this pause point and follow up.
### Scenario 2: Survivor Vaults L Wall
- If you chase a survivor to a TL Wall that allows them to fast vault the L Wall, walk to the end of the L Wall and position your husk there.
- Phase to the pause point. The survivor will either:
- Stand still.
- Medium/Slow vault the window back.
- Move towards you.
- Move towards the T Wall.
- Wrap around the L Wall.
##### If There’s a Connecting Tile/Filler Below 
- For instance, if you chase the survivor into the tile from the left side and there’s a filler pallet below this tile, you’ll want to change your husk placement to zone them away from the filler or cut them off before they can reach it safely. 
- Stand still.
- Instantly vault the window back. 
- Move towards the center of the tile.
- However, if the survivor has enough distance to make the T Wall before you start phasing, set up your husk at the edge of the T Wall and utilize this other pause point instead.
- Stand still on the corner.
- Double back around the corner to fast vault the T Wall window.
- Wrap around the outside of the T Wall.
- Leave the tile.
- If you chase a survivor to a TL Wall that allows them to fast vault the L Wall, begin by positioning your husk at the end of the T Wall.
- Phase to the pause point at the end of the L Wall. The survivor will either:
- Stand still.
- Move towards you.
- Wrap around the L wall.
- Leave the tile.
- For instance, if you chase the survivor into the tile from the right side and there’s a filler pallet below this tile, you’ll want to change your husk placement to zone them away from the filler or cut them off before they can reach it safely. 
- Stand still.
- Vault the window back. 
- Move away from you.
- Stand still.
- Move towards the T Wall window.
- You should be able to hit them before they’re able to make the window, but this will depend on how much distance they have on you.
- Move to wrap around the outside of the T Wall.

- Move towards the window and move up along the wall (instead of vaulting).
- Move towards you.
- It’s very likely that the survivor will either do e) or f), expecting you to follow their pathing. This pause point, however, allows you to save power usage and catch up to them faster if they do decide to do e) or f). If they do any of the other options, it’s also a good spot to pause to be able to hear every outcome and still be able to catch up.

- Wrap around again to the now blocked pallet.

- Stand still
- Double back to the now blocked pallet

- Vault the window

- Leave the tile

- Stand still
- Move toward the top of the tile

See Scenario 1, Example B if they go back into the tile.

## Labyrinth Gyms
### Pallet Control
#### Scenario 1: Survivor Inside of Tile
- This is one of the most annoying tiles to deal with because even if you block the pallet with your husk, the window is still pretty easily playable for the survivor. In this scenario, you have to bait the survivor so you can catch them on either side of the window. 
- Begin by setting up your husk to block the pallet. See How to Block Pallets.
- Phase to the pause point and listen.
- If the survivor stands still/hesitates before vaulting, you can come out and hit them.
- If the survivor tries to move away from the window, you can come out and hit them
- If the survivor immediately fast vaults the window, phase to the second pause point and listen. This pause point will make the survivor think you’re committing all the way around, often leading to them panicking vaulting the window back. If not, you’re still in a good position to react to whatever the survivor does decide to do. 
- Once you phase to the second pause point, pause and listen. The survivor will either:
- Stand still
- Vault the window back
- At this point, they’ll only be able to medium or slow vault the window if they want to immediately vault back, so you’ll always be able to hit them through it. If they swing out to get the fast vault back, the extra time it takes them to swing out will also allow you to hit them through it.

- Move towards you

### No Pallet Control
- If you do not have pallet control, try to bait the survivor into either a) giving you pallet control or b) getting hit through the pallet. You can do this by pretending like you’re going to phase from outside the pallet. Stand still for a couple seconds and observe the survivor.

- If the survivor continues to camp the pallet, start phasing and short swing through the pallet. Either a) you’ll hit the survivor through the pallet, b) you’ll body block the pallet and will be able to swing again to hit the survivor, or c) the survivor will stun you with the pallet.

### Pallet Dropped
- If the survivor is on the inside of the tile and you’re on the outside, just break this pallet before you start phasing. - If the survivor is on the outside of the tile and you’re on the inside, just break this pallet before you start phasing. Much like in Scenario 1, even if you set up your husk to block it and phase around, the survivor will very easily be able to react to which side you’re coming from, and move the opposite way or to a connecting tile/filler. 
- Remember: Be conscientious of what other resources are around this tile before breaking the pallet this way. If the survivor will be able to easily move to another tile/filler pallet, be sure to zone the survivor by walking around to the outside of the tile before breaking this pallet.

### No Pallet Control
#### Scenario 1: Enter From Top of Shack
- This is one of my favourite scenarios to mindgame. If the survivor knows about it, they might not fall for it, but very few survivors know about it and even if they do, they’re forced to play a 50/50 on whether you’re midgaming or actually phasing around Shack since it sounds identical from their pov.
- If the survivor is camping the undropped pallet and you’re at the top of Shack, begin by setting up your husk in the doorway.

- Drop the pallet from the inside, thinking that you’re phasing around the outside of Shack. From here, you can come out of phase and hit them.
- Drop the pallet from the outside, either to call your bluff that you’re not actually on the outside or because they’re not actually listening. From here, you’ll have to just break the pallet.

##### Example B: Survivor Plays Short Wall

#### Scenario 2: Enter from Top Side of Tile
- If you chase a survivor into the tile from the top side and they give you pallet control (i.e., greed the pallet), begin by setting up your husk to properly block the pallet. See How to Block Pallets.
- Phase to the pause point about halfway up the stairs and listen. The survivor will either:
- Stand still.
- Vault the window.
- Move into the corner.
- Be patient. 
- Remember this window can only be vaulted one way, so the survivor will be more patient on vaulting it but they typically will. Don’t accidentally come out of phase too early and zone yourself inside of the crane without getting the hit. Only come out of phase inside the crane if the survivor is absolutely refusing to vault and you know you can hit them from the inside.
### No Pallet Control
- If the survivor chooses to stop camping the pallet and greed it instead (not drop it), move into the pallet and refer to Pallet Control, Scenario 2.
- In my opinion, it’s worth to risk getting stunned here because if you walk the survivor down, you’ll just have to break the pallet anyway. See Pallet Dropped, Scenario 2.
### Pallet Dropped 
- Because the survivor can’t get to the window in this scenario, you can play this almost identically to Pallet Gym, Pallet Dropped. 
- If you chase a survivor into the tile from the bottom side and the survivor is on the opposite side of the dropped pallet, begin by setting up your husk to properly block the pallet. See How to Block Pallets. 
- Phase around the short side to the pause point and listen. The survivor will either:
- Stand still.
- Move towards you.
- Move along the long side of the tile.
- Leave the tile.
- This is one of the scenarios where its best to just break the pallet. If the survivor is on the bottom side of this dropped pallet, they can easily play the window alongside it. In other words, if you set up your husk to block the pallet, phase all the way around the short wall and up the stairs just to get the survivor to vault, you’ll have to use basically the rest of your phase just to get back to the other side of the vault.

## Double Pallet Fillers
- This is the only kind of filler pallet I want to single out from the other filler pallets, as you need to play these slightly different.
- Double Pallet Fillers are strong against Spirit when played correctly if both pallets still exist. So, the best way to play this is to walk the survivor down (without getting stunned) and force them to drop one of the two pallets. Once they do, break it. 
- If you try to play around Double Pallet Fillers with both pallets standing, you’re forcing yourself into a 50/50 on whether you’ll get the hit or not. You can only ever have pallet control of one pallet, and you can only ever block a singular dropped pallet with your husk, so the second pallet will always be free to play by the survivor. 
- This filler becomes very easy to play once one of the pallets is gone, regardless of if you have pallet control or not. So, once you’ve broken one of the pallets, you can play this identically to a normal Filler Pallet.w
## Filler Pallets
- You can play almost every filler pallet the exact same. Each one will be similar to how you would play a Pallet Gym, for instance.
- The only filler pallets you cannot play around are Spirit God pallets. As I explained at the beginning of the guide, Spirit can play around a lot of pallets that would be classified as ‘God pallets’ to other killers (i.e., God Rocks on Macmillan), but there are some that she cannot.
- Spirit God Pallet: A God pallet for spirit classifies as a pallet that Spirit is not able to phase to the other side of and back to where she left her husk. This is because, even if she sets up her husk to block the pallet from one side and is able to phase to the other side of the pallet (forcing the survivor to vault it and become stuck between her husk and the pallet), the survivor will be able to become unstuck before she is able to get back to them, allowing the survivor to freely vault the pallet again or potentially move to another resource. 
- If a survivor throws a God pallet, you must break it. Don’t waste time (or your power) trying to play around it.
### Pallet Control
- If you chase a survivor to a Filler Pallet and they give you pallet control (i.e., greed the pallet), begin by setting up your husk to properly block the pallet. See How to Block Pallets.
- Each pause point will be slightly different depending on the filler, but just as we’ve seen with all of the varying tiles, you should always phase around the shortest side of the filler if possible (i.e., whatever path that will get you to the survivor’s location the fastest) and pause/listen there. The survivor will either:
- Stand still.
- Move towards you.
- Move away from you.
- Leave the tile.
- Remember: It’s important to be patient, listen, and react to where the survivor is and what they’re doing before coming out of phase. If you’re husk is properly blocking a pallet, for instance, and you choose to short phase and/or come out too early, you run the risk of them making it back to the pallet without getting the hit. 
### No Pallet Control
#### Not a God Pallet
- If the filler pallet is not a god pallet and you do not have pallet control, walk down the survivor and force them to drop the pallet without getting stunned.
#### God Pallet
- If the filler pallet is a god pallet and you do not have pallet control, you can try to bait the survivor into either a) giving you pallet control or b) getting hit through the pallet by pretending like you’re going to phase (and making the survivor hesitate/think you’re going to try to phase around). Stand still for a couple seconds and observe the survivor. 
- If there are not any other close tiles/filler pallets nearby, it’s worth to risk getting stunned here because if you walk the survivor down, you’ll just have to break the pallet anyway. 
### Pallet Dropped
- If the survivor is on the opposite side of the dropped filler pallet, begin by setting up your husk to properly block the dropped pallet. See How to Block Pallets. 
- Stand still.
- Move towards you.
- Move away from you.
- Leave the tile.
- Remember: It’s important to be patient, listen, and react to where the survivor is and what they’re doing before coming out of phase. If you’re husk is properly blocking a pallet, for instance, and you choose to short phase and/or come out too early, you run the risk of them being able to play the dropped pallet rather than getting, what should be, a guaranteed hit.
